Turning Long Training Videos into an Onboarding Clip Glossary

Most teams already have hours of recorded training: product demos, tool walkthroughs, all-hands recordings and the occasional screen share someone saved. New hires are told to "watch the recordings", and almost nobody does, because nobody can find the three minutes they need inside a ninety-minute video.

A clip glossary fixes that. It is a simple table where each row is one term or task, linked to a short clip that explains just that thing. It takes an afternoon to build and saves every new person hours.

Step 1: list the words new people ask about

Ask the last two or three people who joined which internal words or tasks confused them in week one. Typical answers are internal tool names, how a request moves from one team to another, where reports live, and how to book something. Aim for 15 to 25 entries. More than that and the glossary becomes another thing nobody reads.

Step 2: find each answer inside the recordings

Skim the recordings at double speed and note the start and end time where each term is explained well. You are looking for the clearest 30 to 120 seconds, not the first mention. Write the times straight into the table as you go.

Step 3: cut the clips

If the recordings are in a video editor or on a shared drive, trim them there. If they live on an unlisted YouTube channel, which is common for internal training, a browser trimmer is quicker than opening an editor. Clipavideo takes the video link plus a start and end time and downloads only that segment, so each row of the table becomes one file in a couple of minutes.

Cut a second or two before the explanation starts and stop right after the key sentence. Clips that begin mid-sentence feel broken, and clips that run on into the next topic confuse people.

Step 4: store them where people already look

Put the table on the team wiki or onboarding page, with the clip files in one shared folder. Keep the original recording link in each row so anyone can watch the surrounding context if they want more.

Step 5: keep it alive

Ask each new hire to flag one missing term and one outdated clip at the end of their first month. Re-cut clips when a tool's interface changes. A glossary that shows old screens erodes trust quickly.
